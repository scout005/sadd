1. Product Vision

Give every household enterprise-grade network visibility and protection — without a subscription, without a networking degree, and without hiding why something was blocked.

Sentinel competes directly with Firewalla by matching its core security stack, and differentiates on transparency, simplicity of the hardware lineup, integrated Wi-Fi, and reduced "active management" burden, per the findings in the competitive analysis.

2. Personas

Persona Description Primary Needs
P1 — Nervous Nadia Non-technical parent, 2–3 kids, smart TV + phones + a few IoT gadgets. Bought the device because a friend recommended it "for the kids." Dead-simple setup, parental controls, doesn't want to touch VLANs ever. [Needs-analysis merge] The household's de facto IT department: content filtering by age category, scheduled internet pauses (bedtime/mealtime), per-kid profiles with a one-tap pause button, usage dashboards (screen time, top categories), automated malicious-domain/phishing blocking, IoT isolation from family devices, mobile-app-first setup and troubleshooting, "it just works" reliability with minimal reboots, and a guest network for visitors/babysitters.
P2 — IoT Ivan Enthusiast homeowner, 40+ connected devices (cameras, sensors, smart plugs), some technical skill. Segmentation, device visibility, IoT isolation, moderate VPN use. [Needs-analysis merge] Tri-band/Wi-Fi 6E-7 capacity to keep 50+ sensors stable on 2.4 GHz while offloading heavy devices to 5/6 GHz, support for 100+ connected devices without degradation, a dedicated isolated IoT SSID/VLAN so smart bulbs/plugs can't reach personal PCs, Thread/Matter/Zigbee hub compatibility, and mesh expandability with seamless roaming (802.11k/v/r or EasyMesh) across nodes.
P3 — Remote-Worker Rae Works from home, handles sensitive company data, uses a company VPN. Stable multi-WAN failover, guest network isolation, secure remote access to home network, minimal latency. [Needs-analysis merge] Application-aware QoS that prioritizes Zoom/Teams/VPN traffic over background downloads, strong upload speed and low latency for video calls, secondary 5G/4G or fiber failover to avoid downtime mid-meeting, VPN support (client and/or server) for remote access into the home network, and VLAN segmentation to isolate corporate devices from untrusted smart-home gear.
P4 — Power-User Priya Home-labber / sysadmin, self-hosts services, wants full control. API access, Docker/container support, advanced routing, open logs, self-hosted option.
P5 — Small-Office Sam Runs a 5–15 person small business/office from a converted space. Multi-site management (MSP-style), compliance-friendly reporting, more device headroom. [Needs-analysis merge] Static IP or reliable DDNS for hosting business-facing services (POS, booking calendar), multi-WAN failover since downtime has direct revenue impact, a separate network for point-of-sale/payment devices isolated from personal/family devices (often a PCI-DSS compliance requirement), guest Wi-Fi for customers kept separate from business-critical systems, and bandwidth prioritization for business traffic during business hours.
P6 — [NEW] Compliance-Conscious Carl Retail buyer/procurement lead (or a security-literate consumer) who explicitly checks country-of-origin and supply-chain documentation before purchasing, post-FCC Covered List rule. Clear "designed/made in USA" or equivalent compliance claim, visible HBOM/SBOM or equivalent trust signal, confidence the product won't be pulled from the market by future regulation.
P7 — [Needs-analysis merge] Gamer Gavin Hardcore gamer/tech enthusiast chasing deterministic performance — zero latency spikes, maximum control. Low-jitter/ping prioritization via QoS, stable connection during peak household usage (no lag spikes from parallel streaming), multi-gig Ethernet (2.5G/10G) and wired backhaul for a dedicated gaming rig, simple port forwarding/UPnP for game servers and NAT issues, VPN client/server support, and detailed network telemetry.
P8 — [Needs-analysis merge] Teen Tyler Teen/kid user — fast, uninterrupted access, largely indifferent to admin/security settings and occasionally motivated to circumvent them. Reliable Wi-Fi reach into bedrooms (dead zones are the #1 complaint), low latency for gaming/streaming with friends, ability to earn extra time/access through reward-based systems rather than pure restriction, a personal device profile so usage isn't blamed on/mixed with siblings', and fair, transparent limits — kids increasingly notice when controls feel opaque or excessive.
P9 — [Needs-analysis merge] Binge-Watching Bea Streaming/media household running multiple simultaneous 4K/HD streams without buffering. Strong 5/6 GHz throughput to the entertainment center, MU-MIMO/OFDMA to handle many simultaneous devices without slowdown, consistent performance during peak evening hours when the whole household streams at once, and QoS that prevents one 4K stream or large download from starving others.
P10 — [Needs-analysis merge] Away Aaron Away-from-home user who needs to securely reach and control cameras, smart locks, thermostats, and sensors from anywhere without exposing the home to the open internet. Live view/playback/alerts from a phone or desktop app with a low-bandwidth mode for mobile data, push notifications for motion/person/package detection, geofencing and automations that don't depend on being physically present, and — the router-relevant decision — a built-in, easy-to-enable VPN server plus DDNS support so remote access doesn't depend on port forwarding or a third-party vendor cloud, with cameras/IoT on an isolated VLAN so a compromised device can't pivot to the rest of the network.
P11 — [Needs-analysis merge] Pet-Parent Paula Pet owner relying on always-on, low-latency connectivity for cameras, feeders, and trackers, often overlapping with Away Aaron (P10) but motivated by pet care rather than general home monitoring. Uptime matters more than raw speed since devices run unattended for hours (a dropped connection means a missed feeding or a blind spot), reliable low-power/low-bandwidth IoT support for battery-powered gadgets, and the same remote-access considerations as P10 (cloud relay vs. VPN).
P12 — [Needs-analysis merge] Set-and-Forget Stan Non-tech-savvy user or senior who wants "set it and forget it" reliability with zero maintenance. Mobile-app-guided setup with QR codes (no browser-based 192.168.1.1 config), automated background firmware updates with no user intervention, self-healing behavior (automatic channel switching, self-reboot on degraded connection), clear app guidance or phone support when issues arise, and larger text/high-contrast UI with voice-guided or screen-reader-compatible setup flows for visually impaired users.
P13 — [Needs-analysis merge] Local-First Layla Privacy/security-conscious user focused on data protection and what the router vendor itself does with usage data — distinct from P6 Carl's supply-chain/compliance focus. WPA3 encryption and regular automatic firmware updates, built-in malware/threat blocking, no data-harvesting practices from the router vendor, the option to run her own DNS/VPN or use open-source firmware, and a strong preference for a router that works fully on the local network without a mandatory cloud-account sign-up.
P14 — [Needs-analysis merge] Multi-Gen Maria Large or multi-generational household needing whole-home coverage without performance loss as user count grows. Mesh systems eliminating dead zones, band steering that auto-connects devices to the best band, enough capacity for many concurrent users without degradation, and multiple SSIDs/family zones (e.g. a separate network for an in-law suite).
P15 — [Needs-analysis merge] Roommate Robin Shared-housing roommate wanting fair, neutral access without one person acting as network administrator over the others. Flat, guest-style access rather than a parent/child control hierarchy, per-device usage visibility for fair cost-splitting, each roommate's browsing/device activity isolated from the others' view, a simple guest network for one roommate's visitors that doesn't require asking the others, and easy device removal/network reset on move-out.
P16 — [Needs-analysis merge] Renter Reese Apartment dweller needing portability and resilience in a dense, interference-prone environment. Compact form factor with no wall-mounting or cabling, easy to take when moving, and automatic/manual channel management to handle neighboring-network congestion common in apartment buildings.
P17 — [Needs-analysis merge] Vacation-Home Vic Multi-property/vacation-home owner monitoring and controlling a property they aren't physically present at for extended stretches — often the actual primary motivation behind a "smart router" purchase. Network-level alerts if the property loses internet or power, the ability to remotely reboot the router without being on-site, cellular (LTE) failover so a local ISP outage doesn't cut off monitoring, time-limited or auto-expiring guest credentials tied to booking dates for short-term rentals, and simple credential rotation between guests without needing to be on-site.

4. Product Backlog (by Epic)

Format: US-# — As a [persona], I want [capability], so that [benefit]. Priority: P0 (MVP-blocking) / P1 (fast-follow) / P2 (roadmap) — MoSCoW-style. Estimate: Story points (Fibonacci: 1,2,3,5,8,13).

P0 5
US-1.11 [Engineering backlog: US3.4] As a power user, I want to require MFA re-authentication for every sensitive admin action (not just login), so that critical changes are extra-protected. Router name requested during local setup, changeable anytime after; a name must be set before a second router can be linked; account home screen lists all linked routers by name with a status indicator; switching reuses the same login/MFA session; each router's devices/profiles/settings stay fully isolated from the others. ID Story Acceptance Criteria Priority Points

[Engineering backlog: US4.5] At minimum, a power user can assign a device to an isolated segment directly from that device's detail screen, with at least one isolated segment available beyond the main and guest networks, and the isolated segment cannot see main-network traffic by default. P0 ~~13~~ 8
US-3.2 As Nadia, I want a one-tap "Kid Zone" and "IoT Zone" preset that auto-segments common device types, so that I get segmentation benefits without configuring VLANs myself. Presets detect device type via fingerprinting and suggest zone assignment; one-tap accept. P0 8
US-3.3 As Ivan, I want device-level microsegmentation so that even devices on the same VLAN can't talk to each other unless explicitly allowed. Default-deny lateral traffic between devices in the same segment unless an allow-rule exists. P1 8
US-3.4 As Rae, I want a fully isolated guest network with its own captive portal / QR-code Wi-Fi sharing, so that visitors never touch my main LAN. QR-generated guest access; guest segment cannot reach any other segment by default. [Engineering backlog: US9.1–9.2] Guest network is reachable as a one-tap toggle from the home dashboard, not only from advanced setup; name/password are auto-generated but editable; user can optionally select an expiration duration (e.g., 24 hours, 1 week) at creation, after which the guest network disables itself automatically. P0 5
US-3.5 As Priya, I want to define Zero Trust least-privilege rules per device/user, so that nothing is trusted by default even inside my LAN. Rule engine supports allow-list-only mode per segment. P1 8
US-3.6 [NEW] As any user, I want my devices correctly recognized even when my phone/laptop uses MAC address randomization (an OS-level privacy feature), so that I don't get falsely flagged as a "new device" every time I reconnect, and I'm never pushed to disable a privacy feature to make the security device work. Device identity uses a combination of behavioral/traffic fingerprinting and MAC, tolerant of rotating MAC addresses, without requiring the user to disable OS privacy features (directly addresses a documented Firewalla UX/privacy contradiction). P0 13

US-4.3 As Priya, I want to search/filter logs by device, category, and time range, so that I can debug connectivity issues quickly. Filterable log UI in both app and local web UI. P1 5
US-4.4 As any user, I want a plain-language explanation when a device or site I expect to work is blocked, with a one-tap "allow" override, so that I can self-resolve false positives without contacting support. "Troubleshoot this block" flow surfaces the exact rule and an inline unblock action. P0 5
US-4.5 As Nadia, I want a simple weekly "Network Health" summary (in plain language, not jargon), so that I understand what my device did for me without reading logs. Push/email digest: threats blocked, top data users, new devices, one actionable tip. P1 5
US-4.6 [Engineering backlog: US2.1, US2.3] As a homeowner, I want a single plain-language security status (Green/Yellow/Red plus a one-line reason) and an at-a-glance summary (connected device count, guest network status, last update date) on the home screen, so I know at a glance if my network is safe without digging for information. Status recomputed on change and on app open; each contributing issue maps to exactly one "Fix" action executing without menu navigation; if the daemon is unreachable the status shows an explicit "can't reach your router" state, never a false green; each summary item is tappable for more detail. P0 5

Category list ≥15 categories; assignable per device or per user profile. [Engineering backlog: US5.1] Enforced at the network level (DNS/SNI), so it cannot be bypassed by uninstalling an app on the child's device — the core differentiator versus app-based controls. P0 8
US-5.2 As Nadia, I want to enforce Safe Search across major search engines and video platforms, so that inappropriate content doesn't surface even in search results. SafeSearch enforced for Google, Bing, YouTube, DuckDuckGo by default when category filter is ON. P0 5
US-5.3 As Nadia, I want to set screen-time schedules (e.g., no internet after 9pm) per device or person, so that I can manage bedtime routines without confiscating devices. Schedule engine supports recurring time windows per device/user/group. P0 5
US-5.4 As Nadia, I want a one-tap "pause internet" button for a specific device or the whole house, so that I can quickly handle dinnertime or homework time. Pause action reachable in ≤2 taps from home screen; auto-resume option. P0 3
US-5.5 As Nadia, I want to assign rules to a person (who may use multiple devices), not just a single device, so that rules follow my child across their phone, tablet, and laptop. "User" object aggregates multiple device MAC/IPs under one identity and rule set. [Engineering backlog: US8.1] Pre-set Kid/Teen profile templates are offered as starting points; profile setup takes fewer than 5 taps. P1 8
US-5.6 As Nadia, I want to see a weekly app/category usage report per child, so that I can have informed conversations about screen time. Auto-generated report, opt-in delivery via app/email. P1 5
US-5.7 [Engineering backlog: US5.2] As a blocked user, I want to see a friendly block page explaining why access was denied, so that I'm not confused by a generic error — with no self-serve "request access" button, since unblocking is a deliberate admin-only action. Block page uses friendly, non-alarming language; no self-serve unblock button is shown; page tells the user to ask the account admin to change this in the app. P0 3
US-5.8 [Engineering backlog: US5.7] As a blocked user on an HTTPS site, I want a clear blocked experience instead of a browser certificate warning, so that the friendly block page actually works in practice. A chosen HTTPS-blocking approach (e.g., connection-reset with app-side explanation, or DNS-level NXDOMAIN with app notification) avoids scary certificate warnings, is tested on iOS/Android/major desktop browsers, and blocked HTTPS attempts remain visible to the admin in-app even when no page can render. P0 3
US-5.9 [Engineering backlog: US5.3] As an admin, I want to unblock a site/app from the app, so that I can grant access when appropriate. Admin can search/select a blocked item and unblock it in two taps or fewer; change takes effect within a few seconds across the network. P1 3
US-5.10 [Engineering backlog: US5.4] As a power user, I want to create custom block lists by domain or IP, so that I can block things not covered by predefined categories. Advanced settings allow manual domain/IP entry for blocking; custom entries can be imported/exported as a list. P2 5
US-5.11 [Engineering backlog: US5.5] As a developer, I want a timeboxed spike evaluating how reliably we can block specific mobile apps (not just websites) at the router level, so that we commit only to what's technically achievable before marketing promises anything. Spike evaluates traffic-fingerprinting against real apps, including CDN-shared infrastructure, certificate pinning, and encrypted SNI/ECH; output is a written report classifying app-blocking capability as reliable / best-effort / not-feasible per category; product messaging and US-5.12's scope are set from this report, not before. 8.2 Sprint 1 — Refined "Ready" Stories (Given/When/Then)

These four right-sized P0 stories are proposed as the Sprint 1 candidate set — chosen because together they deliver one coherent, demoable slice: a new user can unbox the device, get set up, and be automatically protected, with no undisclosed remote-access risk.

US-1.1 — Guided setup in under 10 minutes As Nadia, I want to set up my device in under 10 minutes using only my phone, so that I don't need technical help. - Given a factory-reset Sentinel unit is powered on and connected to an internet uplink, When I open the app and tap "Add Device," Then the app discovers the unit via QR/Bluetooth within 15 seconds. - Given the unit has been discovered, When I complete the guided wizard, Then setup finishes in 6 or fewer screens. - Given setup completes successfully, When the final step finishes, Then I see a "Network Protected" confirmation with a timestamp, and the unit is passing traffic within 60 seconds. - Given I close the app mid-setup, When I reopen it, Then I resume from my last completed step rather than restarting. - Given setup fails at any step (e.g., no uplink detected), When the failure occurs, Then the app shows a specific, plain-language error with a "try again" or "contact support" action — never a raw error code.

US-1.3 — Sensible defaults on by default As any user, I want a guided post-install security wizard that turns on sensible defaults, so that I'm protected without configuring anything manually. - Given US-1.1 has just completed, When the confirmation screen dismisses, Then the defaults wizard launches automatically without a separate user action. - Given the defaults wizard completes, When finished, Then ad-block, new-device quarantine, and baseline IDS/IPS are all enabled unless the user explicitly opts out. - Given a user wants to revisit these choices later, When they go to Settings → Features → Customize, Then the same wizard is re-launchable at any time. - Given a user backs out of the wizard without finishing, When they exit, Then the safe defaults are still applied automatically — skipping the wizard never means skipping protection.

US-2.6 — New device quarantine As Ivan, I want new devices automatically quarantined until I approve them, so that rogue devices can't silently join my network. - Given an unrecognized device joins the network, When it requests a DHCP lease, Then it is placed in a quarantine segment with internet access blocked before any user action is needed. - Given a device is quarantined, When the event occurs, Then a push notification reaches the primary user within 5 seconds, containing device name (best-guess), manufacturer, MAC, and a one-tap Approve/Deny action. - Given the user taps Approve, When processed, Then the device moves to its assigned segment within 3 seconds without needing to reconnect. - Given the user denies or ignores the prompt for 24 hours, When the timeout elapses, Then the device stays quarantined and a daily reminder is sent until resolved. - Given quarantine is toggled off in Settings, When disabled, Then new devices join the default network directly with no quarantine notifications. - ⚠️ Dependency flag: this story assumes basic network-segment plumbing already exists in firmware. If US-3.1a (split above) hasn't landed yet, US-2.6 is blocked — sequence US-3.1a before or alongside US-2.6 in sprint planning.

US-17.6 — Tiers differ in speed, not security As any user, I want clear documentation that hardware tiers differ in throughput/scale, not core security capability, so that I don't overpay believing a pricier unit is "more secure." - Given a user views the product comparison page, When comparing any two tiers, Then the page explicitly states in plain language that all tiers share the same core security feature set. - Given the comparison page lists differences, When displayed, Then only throughput, port count/speed, and device-count headroom appear as differentiators — no security feature is listed as tier-exclusive unless factually true at launch. - Given a user asks support or the FAQ "does the pricier model protect me better," When answered, Then the documented response confirms tiers differ in capacity only (consistency-checked against live marketing copy at release time).

8.4 Facilitation Script — Backlog Refinement Session (60 min)

Purpose: this session targets the vague/oversized story problem directly — not general backlog grooming.

Time Activity

8.5 Coaching Tip for the Product Owner

Pre-split epics before refinement, don't split live as a group habit. Several P0 stories in this backlog (US-2.1, US-3.1, US-3.6, US-15.2, US-17.5) arrived at refinement already epic-sized. For a team new to Scrum, spending scarce ceremony time teaching the skill of splitting (Section 8.4) is valuable once — but going forward, the PO should partner 1:1 with the tech lead before each refinement session to pre-draft 2-3 candidate slices per large story, so the team's time in refinement goes toward validating and estimating slices, not discovering for the first time that a story is too big. This also surfaces hidden dependencies (like US-2.6 → US-3.1a in Section 8.3) earlier, when they're cheap to resequence, instead of mid-sprint.
